# Freight Tracker

A simple web app that a small freight brokerage can use instead of spreadsheets.

## What it does

When the project is finished, the owner or staff can:

- Keep a list of customers (shippers), with their contact details and history.
- Keep a list of trucking companies (carriers) they can use.
- Log every load, from first contact to final delivery.
- Set a status on each load, such as "quoted," "booked," "in transit," or "delivered," so everyone knows where it stands.
- See all open deals and loads on one screen, instead of checking several spreadsheets.
- Filter the list, for example to show only loads that are still in transit.

A typical morning: open the app and see which customers are waiting for a price, which loads still need a truck, which trucks are on the road, and which deliveries are done.

## Glossary: freight terms

- **Broker:** A middleman. You find a customer who needs goods moved, and you find a truck company to move them. You don't own trucks.
- **Shipper:** The customer. The business that has goods to send.
- **Carrier:** The truck company that actually drives the goods.
- **Load:** One job. One shipment going from one place to another.
- **Lane:** A route you use often, like Dallas to Atlanta.
- **Tender:** The customer asks you to handle a load.
- **Cover a load:** Find a truck and driver to take the load.
- **Margin:** Your profit. What the customer pays you, minus what you pay the carrier.
- **Spot rate:** A price for one load, just for now.
- **Contract rate:** A price agreed for a longer time, like several months.
- **Rate confirmation (rate con):** The paper that states the agreed price and terms for one load.
- **Bill of lading (BOL):** The paper the driver gets when the goods are picked up. It lists what was loaded.
- **POD (proof of delivery):** A paper, signed when the goods arrive. It proves the job is done.
- **MC or DOT number:** A carrier's official ID number. You check it before you hire that carrier.
- **Deadhead:** Driving with an empty truck, with no goods.
- **Pipeline:** The steps a customer goes through, from "first contact" to "we do business."
